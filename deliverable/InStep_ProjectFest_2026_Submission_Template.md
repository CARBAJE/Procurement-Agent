## Slide 3 — Section 2: What You Built

> Descripción introductoria: *"Describe your project concretely. Not the pitch — the actual thing you produced."*

| Campo | Pregunta guía |
|---|---|
| **THE PROBLEM YOU WERE SOLVING** | What pain point, gap, or opportunity did your project address? |
| **WHAT YOU ACTUALLY DELIVERED** | What can someone see, use, run, or read? Be specific. |
| **TECHNOLOGIES, METHODS & TOOLS USED** | Key stack, frameworks, methodologies, datasets, or approaches. |
| **WHAT IS STILL IN PROGRESS OR NOT YET COMPLETE** | Be honest. What was scoped but unfinished, and why? |

> 💡 **Nota adicional en la diapositiva:** "Note any key assumptions or scope boundaries your team agreed with your mentor."

> **Tip mostrado en la diapositiva:** *"Evaluators value honesty about what's incomplete over overselling."*

## Slide 5 — Section 4: Where It's Going Next

> Introducción: *"Your work doesn't have to end when your internship does. Tell us what a realistic next chapter looks like."*

Pasos numerados:

1. **IMMEDIATE NEXT STEPS** — The first 1–3 things the next person or team should do.
2. **LONGER-TERM POTENTIAL** — What could this become in 6–12 months, realistically?
3. **WHAT IT NEEDS TO SCALE** — Resources, access, approvals, or integrations required.

Campos adicionales (sin numeración de paso):

| Campo | Pregunta guía |
|---|---|
| **WHO SHOULD OWN THIS AFTER YOU?** | The team, role, or unit best placed to continue this work. |
| **ONE THING THAT WOULD UNLOCK THIS** | If one door opened — what would it be? Access, budget, time, approval? |

## Slide 6 — Section 5: How You Worked

> Introducción: *"Evaluators assess not just what you produced, but how you operated. Reflect honestly on your process."*

| Campo | Pregunta guía |
|---|---|
| **WHAT YOU DROVE YOURSELF vs. WHAT YOU WERE GUIDED THROUGH** | Be clear about where you took initiative and where your mentor's direction was essential. Both are fine — clarity is what matters. |
| **A PROBLEM YOU HIT AND HOW YOU SOLVED IT** | Describe a real moment where something broke, blocked, or surprised you — and what you did about it. |
| **WHAT YOU'D DO DIFFERENTLY** | Hindsight is useful. What would you change about your approach, and what would you keep exactly the same? |

> 🚧 **EXTERNAL CONSTRAINTS OR BLOCKERS** — Access restrictions, late data, scope changes outside your control. *Your mentor will flag these — they won't count against you.*

## Notas de relación con el otro documento (Evaluation Mailer)

- La lista de **7 categorías de proyecto** (Slide 2) es idéntica en fondo a las categorías descritas en el mailer de evaluación (`InStep_ProjectFest_2026_Evaluation_Mailer`), aunque los nombres cortos usados aquí ("Cat 1"… "Cat 7") difieren ligeramente en redacción de los nombres usados en la tabla de mapeo bucket↔categoría del mailer (p. ej. "Living Lab / Robotics / Phygital" aquí vs. "Living lab, robotics, or phygital experience" allá). Se mantiene la redacción exacta de cada documento fuente.
- La **Sección 6 (What You're Submitting)** de esta plantilla corresponde directamente a la sección "What to Bring for Evaluation" del mailer, con los mismos 5 ítems principales (íconos y textos coincidentes). El mailer añade dos ítems adicionales que **no aparecen** en esta plantilla: 💬 *Stakeholder Feedback* y 📊 *Measured Outcome (if applicable)* — aunque "Stakeholder Feedback" sí aparece como campo separado en la Slide 4 de esta plantilla (Section 3), no como parte de la lista de "cosas a incluir en el envío".
- El bloque 🚧 **External Constraints or Blockers** (Slide 6) corresponde directamente al principio de equidad "External constraints noted" del mailer (sección de "Fairness Principles").
- Esta plantilla **no contiene** un campo de declaración de "Evaluation Bucket" (la segunda declaración obligatoria descrita en el mailer). Solo declara "Project Category" (Slide 2). Se documenta como observación de posible inconsistencia entre ambos artefactos, sin asumir en qué parte del formulario debería añadirse.
